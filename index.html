01 · Executive Problem Statement & Analytical Breakthrough

Solving 70 Years of Scoring Inflation & Heterogeneous Race Telemetry

Between 1950 and 2010, the FIA inflated the points awarded for a Grand Prix victory by 312.5% (from 8 points to 25 points) and expanded seasons from 7 races to 22+. Naive career totals make modern midfielders look statistically superior to Ayrton Senna or Alain Prost — until normalized inside an ACID Medallion Lakehouse.

The Engineering Challenge

Formula 1 data arrives after every Grand Prix weekend in eight distinct shapes: flat CSVs (circuits, races), nested JSON objects (drivers.json with name STRUCT<forename, surname>), multi-line JSON (pit_stops.json), and multi-file split folders (lap_times/ and qualifying/).

Re-running full historical loads every Sunday evening wastes cluster compute, while blind appends duplicate race results if a job retries. Our lakehouse solves this with parameterized file_date cutovers and Delta Lake MERGE INTO.

Stage 01: Azure Data Factory Master & Gatekeeper Pipelines

The parent pipeline (pl_process_formula1_data) chains ingestion before transformation. Inside pl_ingest_formula1_data, a Get Metadata activity verifies that the race weekend's cutover folder (e.g. 2021-03-28) exists in ADLS Gen2 before an If Condition launches the Databricks job cluster.

Why it matters: Prevents expensive Databricks cluster spin-up when a scheduled trigger fires on a non-race weekend.

All-Time Dominant Drivers — Normalized Gold Layer (`AVG(11 - position)`)

Every P1 victory equals 10.0 points regardless of whether it occurred in 1955, 1988, or 2020. Legends from the 8-point and 9-point eras compete on equal mathematical footing.

GOLD LAYER SPARK SQL QUERY EXECUTED

-- Formula1/analysis/1.find_dominant_driver.sql SELECT driver_name, COUNT(1) AS number_of_races, SUM(calculated_points) AS total_points, ROUND(AVG(calculated_points), 2) AS average_points FROM f1_gold.calculated_race_results GROUP BY driver_name HAVING COUNT(1) >= 50 ORDER BY average_points DESC;
04 · Production Engineering Rigor

How the lakehouse handles 1950s FIA shared-car rules, strict schema enforcement, and upstream API continuity.

DOMAIN EDGE CASE SOLVED

1950s Shared-Car Deduplication

In 1950s Formula 1, two drivers could share one car in the same Grand Prix, producing duplicate (driver_id, race_id) keys in raw results.json that violate Delta Lake's MERGE INTO uniqueness constraint.

Solved by enforcing dropDuplicates(["driver_id", "race_id"]) in 5.ingest_results_file.py prior to executing the Delta merge.

SCHEMA ENFORCEMENT

Qualifying Split-JSON & Merge Library

Enforces explicit StructType schemas across all 8 ingestion pipelines, preserving formatted qualifying lap times ("1:26.572") as StringType() in 8.ingest_qualifying_file.py.

Centralizes Dynamic Partition Pruning and Delta upserts inside Formula1/includes/common_functions.py.

UPSTREAM CONTINUITY

Jolpica-F1 & Unity Catalog Ready

Decouples the Bronze ADLS Gen2 landing zone from the upstream HTTP provider so the lakehouse transitions seamlessly from the retired Ergast API to Jolpica-F1 and FastF1 3.7 Hz telemetry under Unity Catalog External Locations.
